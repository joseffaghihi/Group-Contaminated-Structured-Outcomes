# Journal fit

## Recommended journal

**Journal of Causal Inference (JCI)**  
Official journal page: <https://www.degruyterbrill.com/journal/key/jci/html>

JCI is the closest match because the paper's primary contribution is a causal
identification result: it characterizes the observable causal target under a
latent group action, identifies the interventional quotient law, and supplies
finite-sample randomization inference.  The image and Euler constructions are
implementations of that causal problem rather than a stand-alone computer
vision or topological-data-analysis contribution.

The journal's official author page accepts LaTeX/AMS-LaTeX and recommends a
single PDF for initial submission.  The supplied manuscript is therefore kept
as portable LaTeX for the initial scientific review; it can be transferred to
the journal class after acceptance or if the editor requests it.

As of 31 July 2026, the official page lists the journal as fully open access,
with no submission fee and an article-processing charge of EUR 1,050 upon
acceptance.  Authors should recheck that amount and waiver eligibility before
submission.

## What makes the submission credible for JCI

- A sharply defined causal observation model and estimand.
- Necessary-and-sufficient observability and maximal-invariant theorems with
  complete proofs and explicit measurability assumptions.
- A finite-sample exact test under a stated randomization mechanism.
- A discovery/confirmation split, a frozen and hash-recorded analysis
  manifest, and explicit records of pre-outcome design corrections.
- Type-I-error and power simulations with exact binomial uncertainty.
- A row-level confirmation experiment on real images, exact invariance gates,
  acquisition-only negative controls, a prespecified secondary-contrast family
  with Holm adjustment, and
  approximate-action stress tests.
- Complete code, progress/ETA reporting, file-integrity checks, and an
  executable reproduction path.

## Alternatives

1. **Electronic Journal of Statistics** is the strongest alternative if the
   final emphasis shifts toward the statistical theory of the kernel test and
   its operating characteristics.
2. **Annals of Applied Statistics** becomes plausible only if the biological
   analysis, rather than the causal identification theorem, becomes the
   organizing scientific contribution.

The present manuscript should not be sent to a general machine-learning
journal without a substantially broader learning-theoretic contribution.

## Assessment of the suggested top-general-statistics venues

The expert suggestion that the current experiments are already exceptionally
strong for JASA, JRSS-B, or Biometrika is not a defensible submission claim.
The primary RxRx1 test has eight blocks, the acquisition nuisance is imposed,
and only one scientific image domain is analyzed. The new exact bounded-odds
sensitivity analysis and persistence-stability theorem strengthen the paper,
but they do not replace independent natural-acquisition validation or a broader
asymptotic/statistical theory. JCI therefore remains the best-matched first
submission rather than a fallback recommendation.
